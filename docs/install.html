Who loses the thread, and how

These are not four versions of one problem. They fail differently, which is why one accommodation never covers them.

Blind and low-vision students

The slide carries its meaning as pixels. A screen reader reaches it and finds nothing to say.

Deaf and hard-of-hearing students

The instructor’s voice carries meaning no slide contains. That channel is simply absent.

Students reading in a second language

Comprehension lags delivery. By the time one sentence is parsed, two more have gone by.

Students with ADHD

Attention lapses, and re-entry is expensive. “What did I miss” has no cheap answer in a live room.

What all four share is re-entry. Everyone loses the thread at some point; the difference is whether getting back in costs a whisper to a neighbour, an email after class, or nothing at all. AccessLens makes it cost nothing: one button returns a short summary of what the instructor covered while you were away.

Install it

Not on the Chrome Web Store — this is a prototype, so it loads unpacked. Chrome or Edge. Two minutes.

Download AccessLens

A .zip you unpack yourself. No account, no sign-in.

  1. Unzip the download.
  2. Open chrome://extensions.
  3. Turn on Developer mode, top right.
  4. Choose Load unpacked and select the unzipped folder.
  5. Open the side panel from the AccessLens toolbar icon.

What it does not do

An accessibility tool that quietly collects things is not an accessibility tool. These are commitments, not omissions.

It cannot start watching by itself

Screen sharing needs the instructor to press Start and pick a source in the browser’s own dialog.

It never records the session

Slides and audio stay on the device they came from. Only the name of the current topic is sent.

It does not judge anyone

No grades, no attention tracking, no guessing at a diagnosis. Your settings stay on your machine.

It says when it is unsure

Shown something it has not been given, it says so rather than inventing a description.
