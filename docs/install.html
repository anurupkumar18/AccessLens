How it works

Three things to do. Nothing to configure.

The instructor keeps teaching from the deck they already made. Students choose how they want to receive it, once.

1

Start the session

Press Start, pick the window with your slides, and read out the join code. Captions start with one more click.

2

Students follow along

They enter the code and pick a mode. The current slide and region arrive automatically, in their format.

3

Drop in your materials

Slides, PDFs, documents, images and recordings come back with alt text and captions already on them.

What it does

Accessible by default, not on request.

Every part of a lesson has a second form: the slide has words, the words have captions, the recording has a transcript, the diagram has a description.

Course materials

Upload anything. It comes back accessible.

PowerPoint, PDF, Word, images, audio and video. Pages are described, figures get alt text, recordings get captions and a transcript that jumps the player. Students open it with your class code.

No review queue, no approval step, nothing to tick off.

Live lesson

Everyone on the same slide

The instructor’s screen is recognised on their own device, and only the name of what’s on it is sent to the class.

Live captions

Captions while you speak

The instructor’s microphone becomes captions on every student’s screen, about five seconds behind the sentence.

Any page

Explain what I’m reading

On a reading, a syllabus or a Canvas page, one button explains or simplifies it — and says so when the page is too thin to explain.

Read and hear

In another language, or aloud

The current part of the lesson, translated or spoken on request — never talking over the instructor.

Catch up

“What did I miss?”

One button returns a short summary of what was covered while you were away, from the same reviewed content.

Why

Who loses the thread, and how

These are not four versions of one problem. They fail differently, which is why one accommodation never covers them.

Blind and low-vision students

The slide carries its meaning as pixels. A screen reader reaches it and finds nothing to say.

Deaf and hard-of-hearing students

The instructor’s voice carries meaning no slide contains. That channel is simply absent.

Students reading in a second language

Comprehension lags delivery. By the time one sentence is parsed, two more have gone by.

Students with ADHD

Attention lapses, and re-entry is expensive. “What did I miss” has no cheap answer in a live room.

What all four share is re-entry. Everyone loses the thread at some point; the difference is whether getting back in costs a whisper to a neighbour, an email after class, or nothing at all. AccessLens makes it cost nothing.

Install

Two minutes, no account.

Not on the Chrome Web Store — this is a prototype, so it loads unpacked. Chrome or Edge. Prefer no install? The web app covers everything except screen sharing and live captions.

Download AccessLens

A .zip you unpack yourself. No account, no sign-in.

Or open the hosted web app.

  1. Unzip the download.
  2. Open chrome://extensions.
  3. Turn on Developer mode, top right.
  4. Choose Load unpacked and select the unzipped folder.
  5. Open the side panel from the AccessLens toolbar icon.

Limits

What it does not do

An accessibility tool that quietly collects things is not an accessibility tool. These are commitments, not omissions.

It cannot start watching by itself

Screen sharing needs the instructor to press Start and pick a source in the browser’s own dialog.

It never records the session

Slides and audio stay on the device they came from. Only the name of the current topic is sent.

It does not judge anyone

No grades, no attention tracking, no guessing at a diagnosis. Your settings stay on your machine.

It says when it is unsure

Shown something it has not been given, it says so rather than inventing a description.
